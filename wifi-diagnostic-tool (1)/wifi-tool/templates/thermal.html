How This Works

  • Camera mode — real camera, styled false-color: your webcam feed is converted to grayscale luminance, then remapped through a thermal color ramp (deep blue → orange → white) frame by frame, with a small on-device model (TensorFlow.js + COCO-SSD) boxing detected people. Everything runs locally in your browser, no frames are sent anywhere. Like any visible-light camera, it needs enough ambient light to see by.
  • WiFi Radar mode — no camera, works day or night: this reuses the exact same heuristic as Presence Watch — a background scan (netsh wlan show networks mode=bssid, via /api/presence) reads a live 0–100% signal-quality figure for every router/access point currently visible nearby, not just the one your laptop is connected to. A person moving through the space tends to add volatility to those readings that a static, empty room doesn't. Because it never touches a camera or ambient light, it reads identically in full daylight or total darkness. It renders as a rotating radar sweep (like an aircraft/ship PPI scope) with 0–2 glowing "contacts" that light up as the beam passes — but their bearing/range is a smooth illustrative drift, and whether they appear at all comes only from the single aggregated motion score, not a real position fix. A signal-quality heuristic can tell you "something's moving nearby," not a measured bearing, range, or headcount — the canvas says so directly.
  • "In range" / signal gating: both modes reuse the live WiFi signal from Diagnostics. Camera mode arms/disarms on it; WiFi Radar mode simply needs an active connection to have anything to measure.
Neither mode is real detection hardware. Camera mode's "range" is a stylistic gate driven by signal quality (%), not a real measurement of distance. WiFi Radar mode is the same coarse, multi-router signal-quality heuristic described in the README (see Notes & limitations), styled to look like a radar scope — it is not a true RF-imaging or WiFi-CSI radar, which needs directional/phased antenna arrays and channel-state access this tool doesn't have. Treat both as a themed, experimental dashboard, not a security or safety device.
